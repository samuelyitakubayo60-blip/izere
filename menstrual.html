Menstrual Health

Understanding your cycle is power. Learn about the menstrual cycle, period tracking, hygiene, and when a visit to the doctor is needed.

🩸 What Is the Menstrual Cycle?

The menstrual cycle is a natural monthly process that prepares the female body for possible pregnancy. It is controlled by hormones and is completely normal.

📆
Normal Cycle
21–35 days
🩸
Bleeding Lasts
3–7 days
🌸
First Period Age
10–15 years
💡 Important to Know
  • Day 1 of the cycle = the first day of bleeding
  • Irregular periods are common in the first 1–2 years after menarche (first period)
  • Every girl's cycle is different — there is no single "correct" cycle length
  • Stress, illness, or major changes in weight can affect cycle regularity

🔄 The 4 Phases of the Cycle

Your cycle has four distinct phases. Understanding them helps you know your body better.

Days 1–5
🩸
Menstrual Phase

Bleeding occurs. The body sheds the uterine lining. Cramps may happen.

Days 1–13
🌱
Follicular Phase

The body prepares an egg. Hormones rise. Energy often increases.

~Day 14
🥚
Ovulation

An egg is released. This is the most fertile period — highest chance of pregnancy.

Days 15–28
🌙
Luteal Phase

Body prepares for pregnancy. PMS symptoms may occur. If no pregnancy → cycle restarts.

🔬 About Ovulation (Most Fertile Day)
  • Ovulation usually occurs around the middle of your cycle
  • The egg lives for only 12–24 hours after release
  • Sperm can survive up to 5 days in the body
  • This means you can get pregnant a few days before or after ovulation
  • Signs of ovulation: mild cramping, clear stretchy discharge, slight temperature rise

💭 PMS — Premenstrual Symptoms

Many girls and women experience physical and emotional changes in the days before their period. This is called PMS (Premenstrual Syndrome).

Physical Symptoms
  • Abdominal cramps or bloating
  • Breast tenderness
  • Headaches
  • Fatigue and low energy
  • Backache
  • Acne breakouts
Emotional Symptoms
  • Mood swings
  • Irritability or sadness
  • Anxiety or feeling overwhelmed
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Changes in appetite or sleep
💪 How to Manage PMS
  • Exercise lightly (walking, stretching) — it helps reduce cramps
  • Apply a warm cloth or hot water bottle to your abdomen
  • Eat iron-rich foods: spinach, beans, meat
  • Avoid caffeine, salt, and sugary foods before your period
  • Rest and sleep well
  • Stay hydrated — drink plenty of water
  • Over-the-counter pain relief (ibuprofen, paracetamol) can help cramps

📱 How to Track Your Period

Tracking your cycle helps you predict your next period, understand your fertility, and notice any health changes.

📅
Calendar Method

Mark the first day of each period on a calendar. Count the days to the next first day — that's your cycle length.

📱
Period Apps

Apps like Clue, Flo, or Period Tracker can help predict your next period and ovulation day.

📝 What to Track Each Month
  • Date period started (Day 1)
  • How many days it lasted
  • How heavy the flow was (light/medium/heavy)
  • Any pain or unusual symptoms
  • Mood changes or energy levels

🧼 Menstrual Hygiene

Good hygiene during your period is important for your health and confidence.

🩹
Sanitary Pads

Most common. Change every 4–6 hours or when soaked.

🌿
Reusable Pads

Washable and eco-friendly. Cost-effective long-term.

🥤
Menstrual Cup

Silicone cup inserted internally. Emptied every 8–12 hours.

✅ Important Hygiene Rules
  • Change your sanitary product every 4–6 hours — don't wait until it's full
  • Wash your hands before and after changing
  • Bathe or shower daily during your period
  • Wash the vaginal area with clean water only — no soap inside
  • Dispose of pads safely (wrap and bin — don't flush)
  • Wear clean, breathable cotton underwear

🏥 When to Seek Medical Help

Most period symptoms are normal, but some signs indicate you should see a healthcare provider.

⚠️ See a Doctor If You Have
  • Very heavy bleeding — soaking more than one pad per hour
  • Periods lasting more than 7 days
  • Severe pain that stops you from doing daily activities
  • No period by age 16 (primary amenorrhea)
  • Period stopped for 3 or more months (and you're not pregnant)
  • Unusual discharge with bad smell
  • Extreme mood changes that affect your daily life
💚 Normal Things NOT to Worry About
  • Mild cramps in the first 1–2 days
  • Slightly irregular periods in the first 1–2 years
  • Small blood clots (smaller than a 5-franc coin)
  • Slight mood changes before your period
  • Period blood that is dark brown (especially at start/end)
