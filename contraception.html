Contraceptive Methods

Understand all your options — advantages, side effects, and how to use them. Information aligned with Rwanda Ministry of Health and WHO guidelines.

Medically Verified Private Reading Rwanda MoH Guidelines

🔵 1. Condoms (Male & Female)

The only method that protects against BOTH pregnancy AND STIs including HIV.

✅ Advantages
  • Protects against STIs and HIV
  • Prevents pregnancy
  • No hormones involved
  • Easily available & affordable
  • Used only when needed
  • Female condoms give women control
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Must be used correctly every time
  • Can break or slip if used wrong
  • Some people find it reduces sensation
  • Requires availability before sex
💡 Correct Use
  • Check the expiry date before use
  • Open carefully — do not use teeth or nails
  • Put on before any sexual contact begins
  • Leave space at the tip
  • Remove carefully after sex before going soft

💊 2. Birth Control Pills

Hormonal pills taken daily to prevent pregnancy. Available at all health centers in Rwanda.

✅ Advantages
  • Very effective when taken correctly
  • Regulates and lightens periods
  • Can reduce menstrual pain and acne
  • Fertility returns quickly after stopping
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Must be taken every day at same time
  • May cause nausea, headaches initially
  • Does NOT protect against STIs
  • Less effective if you forget doses

💉 3. Injectables (3-month shot)

A hormonal injection given every 2–3 months at a health center. Very popular in Rwanda.

✅ Advantages
  • Very effective (99%+)
  • Private — no one can see you using it
  • Lasts 2–3 months per injection
  • May reduce or stop periods
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Requires clinic visits every 3 months
  • May cause irregular bleeding
  • Possible weight gain
  • Fertility may take 6–12 months to return
  • Does NOT protect against STIs

🔩 4. Implants (3–5 years)

A small rod inserted under the skin of the upper arm by a health professional. Very effective long-term option.

✅ Advantages
  • 97–99% effective
  • Long-lasting — 3–5 years
  • Nothing to remember daily
  • Can be removed at any time
  • Fertility returns quickly after removal
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Requires minor insertion procedure
  • May cause irregular bleeding (common)
  • No STI protection
  • Slight arm discomfort initially

🔘 5. IUD / Coil (5–10 years)

A small T-shaped device inserted into the uterus. Two types: copper (non-hormonal) and hormonal.

✅ Advantages
  • Very effective (99%+)
  • Long-lasting: 5–10 years
  • Copper IUD is hormone-free
  • Can be used as emergency contraception
  • Low maintenance after insertion
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Must be inserted by a trained professional
  • May cause discomfort during insertion
  • Copper IUD may increase period flow
  • No STI protection

🚨 6. Emergency Contraception

Also called the "morning-after pill." Taken AFTER unprotected sex to prevent pregnancy. NOT a regular method.

✅ Advantages
  • Prevents pregnancy when used in time
  • Easy to use — one or two pills
  • Available at pharmacies without prescription
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • NOT for regular use
  • Effectiveness decreases with time (best within 24h)
  • May cause nausea, spotting
  • Does NOT end an existing pregnancy
  • No STI protection
⏰ Time is Critical
  • Within 24 hours: up to 95% effective
  • Within 48 hours: up to 85% effective
  • Within 72 hours: up to 75% effective
  • After 72 hours: much less effective — see a doctor

🌿 7. Natural / Calendar Methods

Tracking your cycle to avoid sex on fertile days. Requires a very regular cycle and careful tracking.

✅ Advantages
  • No cost
  • No hormones or side effects
  • Acceptable for religious/cultural reasons
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Less effective (76–88% typical use)
  • Requires careful daily tracking
  • Difficult with irregular periods
  • No STI protection
  • Needs commitment from both partners

⚕️ 8. Permanent Methods (Sterilization)

Surgical procedures for people who are certain they do not want any (or more) children. Consult a doctor.

✅ Advantages
  • Permanent — no ongoing action needed
  • Very effective (>99%)
  • One-time procedure
⚠️ Disadvantages
  • Considered permanent — not reversible
  • Requires surgery
  • No STI protection
  • Requires careful consideration and counseling
